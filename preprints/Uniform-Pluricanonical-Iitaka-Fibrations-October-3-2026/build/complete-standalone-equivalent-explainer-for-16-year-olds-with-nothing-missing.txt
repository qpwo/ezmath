Can the dimension of a shape defined by polynomial equations determine a single pluricanonical degree at which its canonical systems expose the entire Iitaka fibration? Over every algebraically closed field of characteristic zero the proposed answer is yes. The degree depends only on dimension, not on equations, coefficients, or other numerical invariants. No small or practical value is asserted. The argument attributed to OpenAI, dated October 3, 2026, is conditional on four external inputs: Log abundance in characteristic zero (LA, 2026), Theorem 11.1, giving good minimal models in every dimension with nonvanishing; Arithmetic Stein-degree bounds for log Calabi-Yau pairs (SD, 2026), Theorem 1.1, giving an arithmetic degree bound; Uniform effective log Iitaka fibrations for fourfolds (U4, 2026), giving the dimension-independent generic-chain, tracking, covering, diagonal-product, and rational-transport arguments; and Relative denominators and effective systems for log Calabi-Yau fibrations (R4, 2026), giving the curve-denominator and generalized torsion methods. All-dimensional abundance is not derived from finite generation for big adjoints. The conclusion remains conditional on the correctness and applicability of those inputs and the arguments joining them.

A variety is a geometric object defined by polynomial equations. Its dimension counts independent local coordinates at a typical point. Integral means irreducible and reduced: one algebraic piece without nilpotent thickening. Projective means closed in projective space, whose coordinates are ratios; projective complex varieties are compact. Smooth means that suitable local coordinates have no singularity. A field is algebraically closed when every nonconstant one-variable polynomial has a root. Characteristic zero means that adding 1 to itself never gives 0.

A rational function is a quotient of polynomial expressions, defined where the denominator does not vanish. The field k(X) consists of all rational functions on X. A birational map is a rational map with a rational inverse; it identifies dense open pieces and function fields. A fibration groups points into fibres over a lower-dimensional base. Its generic fibre is the fibre over the base function field; the geometric generic fibre also allows algebraic closure of that field. A contraction f:X→Z is projective and surjective with f_*O_X=O_Z. Informally, functions constant along fibres come from Z. For normal varieties in characteristic zero the generic fibre is geometrically integral.

At a smooth point of an n-dimensional variety, a top differential form looks locally like a(x) dx_1∧...∧dx_n. Changing coordinates multiplies the wedge by a Jacobian determinant. These transformation rules define the canonical line bundle O_X(K_X). A line bundle is a family of one-dimensional vector spaces varying algebraically; a section chooses a vector in each fibre according to those rules. An m-canonical, or pluricanonical, form is a section of O_X(mK_X). Here m is the tensor degree, not the degree of a polynomial defining X.

Write H^0(X,mK_X) for the vector space of global m-canonical forms. A basis s_0,...,s_N defines the rational map x↦[s_0(x):...:s_N(x)]. This is the map of the complete system |mK_X|: every section of that degree is used. Where s_0 is nonzero, the map records the rational functions s_i/s_0. Changing basis changes projective coordinates, not the generated field.

If no positive degree has a nonzero section, the Kodaira dimension κ(X) is -∞. Otherwise κ(X) is the largest dimension of these images. Iitaka's theorem says that sufficiently large divisible degrees describe a stable fibration, the Iitaka fibration. Its function field K(K_X) is the subfield of k(X) generated by ratios of sections in the same positive degree, allowing all degrees. The uniform statement is: for each d≥1 there is m(d)>0 such that |m(d)K_X| is nonempty and its ratios generate K(K_X) for every smooth integral projective d-fold X with κ(X)≥0 in characteristic zero.

Equality of fields matters. The fields C(t^2) and C(t) both describe one-dimensional images, but C(t^2) misses t, and the extension has degree two. Merely obtaining an image of dimension κ(X) could miss a finite part of the Iitaka field. If κ(X)=d, the uniform map must be birational. If κ(X)=0, it must be nonempty with image a point.

A prime divisor is an irreducible subvariety of codimension one. A rational divisor is a finite sum D=Σ a_i D_i with rational coefficients. For a rational function h, Div(h) records orders of zero and pole. Such a divisor is principal. Linear equivalence D∼E means D−E is principal; rational linear equivalence D∼_Q E means a positive integral multiple of D−E is principal. Numerical equivalence only compares intersection numbers and is weaker.

A Cartier divisor is locally the divisor of one rational function; it defines a line bundle. A rational Cartier divisor is a rational divisor with some positive integral Cartier multiple. On a normal variety, integral Weil divisors have divisorial sheaves: rational sections satisfying the required inequalities at every prime divisor, even when these sheaves are not line bundles. Normality ensures regularity away from codimension-two subsets suffices. Reflexive differential forms likewise extend from the smooth locus across codimension at least two.

A resolution replaces singularities by a smooth variety through a proper birational map. An exceptional divisor is contracted to codimension at least two. Normalization repairs failures of integral closure without changing the function field. Q-factorial means every integral Weil divisor has a positive Cartier multiple. A small birational modification contracts no divisor. Pullback transports functions, forms, or Cartier divisors along a map; pushforward of divisors sends surviving primes to their images with appropriate degree and discards contracted primes. The symbols f^* and f_* denote these opposite operations.

Intersection numbers count intersections after moving representatives into suitable position, extended by linearity to rational divisors. On a curve, degree counts zeros minus poles of a rational section. The number D^e on an e-fold is the intersection of e copies of D. A boundary component is horizontal over a base if it dominates that base, and vertical otherwise. The notation ⌊D⌋ rounds each coefficient down. A torsion divisor class becomes zero after some positive integer multiple; the uniform problem asks for a single integer for an entire class of varieties.

A pair (X,B) consists of a normal integral variety and an effective boundary B=Σ b_i B_i, with rational coefficients in [0,1] and K_X+B rational Cartier. On a resolution q:W→X write K_W+B_W=q^*(K_X+B). For any prime E on any such model, its log discrepancy is 1−coeff_E(B_W). Log canonical (lc) means all these numbers are nonnegative; Kawamata log terminal (klt) means they are positive. Divisorial log terminal (dlt) is the lc condition with a controlled simple-normal-crossing description around generic boundary strata. For zero boundary, canonical means exceptional discrepancies, log discrepancies minus one, are nonnegative; terminal means positive.

The claimed index statement is: for each d≥0 and rational DCC set Φ⊂[0,1], some integer a(d,Φ)>0 makes a(d,Φ)(K_X+B) an integral principal divisor for every projective normal lc d-fold pair with coefficients in Φ and K_X+B∼_Q 0. DCC, the descending chain condition, excludes infinite strictly decreasing sequences; a DCC set need not be finite, for example {1−1/e:e≥1}. A uniform index means a common trivializing multiple, clearing coefficients and killing the class. A bound on least indices gives a common multiple by taking their least common multiple.

Positivity measures the supply of sections. A divisor is effective if its coefficients are nonnegative; pseudo-effective if its numerical class is a limit of effective classes; nef if it has nonnegative intersection with every curve; ample if a positive multiple embeds the variety; semiample if a positive multiple has no base points and defines a morphism. A divisor D on an e-fold is big when its section count grows like a positive constant times k^e. Its volume is vol(D)=limsup e! h^0(X,kD)/k^e. For a nef big divisor this equals D^e. The notation A⪯B means B−A is rationally linearly equivalent to an effective divisor. Restricting this relation requires avoiding containment in that effective divisor.

Historically, Hacon-McKernan, Takayama, and Tsuji established uniform birational pluricanonical degrees in general type. Hacon-McKernan-Xu extended effective birationality to big lc adjoints with DCC boundary coefficients. Fujino-Mori's canonical bundle formula gave a uniform result for threefolds of Kodaira dimension one. Viehweg-Zhang treated two-dimensional Iitaka bases with dependence on fibre invariants. Birkar-Zhang gave a general effective Iitaka result depending on dimension, a least nonvanishing fibre degree, and a middle Betti number of a smooth model of the fibre's canonical cover. The extra difficulty below general type is controlling fibre torsion and monodromy. The proposed argument bounds the needed characters without assuming uniform bounds on nonabelian middle Betti numbers.

The induction has three assertions in dimension n: the effective Iitaka assertion I(n), the normal lc index assertion L(n), and its zero-boundary klt case K(n). Dimension zero needs degree one. Assuming I(j) and L(j) for j<n, lower-dimensional lc indices control moduli denominators and torsion over rationally connected bases. Separately, lower-dimensional effective Iitaka maps control section orders. These routes combine to prove K(n). Adjunction and an arithmetic field norm then give L(n); moduli denominators and effective birationality on the base give I(n). The denominator argument must not use L(n) before it is proved.

Hacon-McKernan-Xu's global ACC theorem reduces L(n) for a DCC coefficient set to L(n) for a finite subset: coefficients of numerically trivial lc pairs in fixed dimension cannot range freely through that DCC set. ACC is the ascending chain condition. Their ACC for log canonical thresholds also controls discriminant coefficients below. Kollár's Singularities of the Minimal Model Program fixes discrepancy conventions; Kollár-Mori's Birational Geometry of Algebraic Varieties supplies adjunction and the finite discrepancy formula.

The good-model input LA says that a projective lc pair with effective rational boundary and pseudo-effective adjoint has a good log minimal model. Thus the adjoint has a nonzero multiple and becomes semiample on that model. Birkar-Cascini-Hacon-McKernan (BCHM) supplies the klt minimal model program, extractions, and finite generation for big klt adjoints. A minimal model program contracts or flips negative directions. A flip changes the variety in codimension at least two while reversing the relevant relative positivity. A crepant change preserves the pullback of the full canonical adjoint.

A useful descent observation is elementary. If a rational function on a normal projective contraction X→Z has only vertical zeros and poles, its restriction to the generic fibre has none; it is regular there and belongs to k(Z). If K_X+B∼_Q 0 and q(K_{X_η}+B_η)∼0 on the generic fibre, extend a trivializing q-canonical form to a rational form s on X. Then Div(s)+qB=f^*D with D∼_Q 0. Indeed, a suitable power s^a divided by a global trivializing aq-log form is a rational function with vertical divisor. It comes from the base, and division gives D.

Linear triviality on a geometric generic fibre descends in the same degree. If a specified divisorial sheaf becomes an invertible trivial sheaf after algebraic closure, faithful flatness descends invertibility. Base change gives a one-dimensional section space over the original field; any nonzero descended section is a generator because it becomes one after extension. This avoids multiplying by an uncontrolled field-extension degree.

To deduce L(n) from K(n), Xu's klt induction theorem first handles klt pairs with boundary. For a non-klt pair, take a crepant Q-factorial dlt modification and put T=⌊B⌋, adjoining 1 to the finite coefficient set. For small rational ε>0, (X,B−εT) is klt and its adjoint is rationally equivalent to −εT, not pseudo-effective. Its MMP ends in a Mori fibre space, a contraction on which the negative perturbed adjoint is relatively ample.

The unperturbed K_X+B stays crepant through this program. A fixed equality r(K_X+B)=Div(h) pushes through contractions and flips; on common resolutions both pullbacks are Div(h)/r. Thus lc singularities and exact principal multiples persist even if dlt singularities do not. If the Mori base is a point, X is of Fano type. Birkar's bounded complement theorem supplies a bounded complement B^+≥B with c(K_X+B^+)∼0. The difference B^+−B is effective and numerically trivial, hence zero by intersection with an ample class.

If the Mori base Z has positive dimension, T is relatively ample, so a component S dominates Z. Adjunction on its normalization ν:S^ν→S gives (K_X+B)|_{S^ν}=K_{S^ν}+Θ. The pair on S^ν is normal lc, effective, and rationally trivial. Its different coefficients lie in the rational DCC set determined by the original coefficients. Lower-dimensional L and global ACC give a uniform trivializing degree. Choose q clearing the original coefficients and trivializing both this adjoint and the generic-fibre adjoint.

The descended form identity gives Div(s)+qB=f^*D. The q-fold residue along S is a nonzero rational q-canonical form v with Div(v)+qΘ=(f∘ν)^*D. This is an equality of actual divisors; pluricanonical adjunction in a sufficiently divisible tensor power followed by division of orders proves it without assuming Cartierness in degree q everywhere on S. Divide v by a trivializing form v_0. In the Stein factorization S^ν→Y→Z, the first map has connected fibres and the second, h, is finite. Vertical descent places a=v/v_0 in k(Y), and injectivity of pullback gives h^*D=Div_Y(a). The field norm then gives (deg h)D=Div_Z(Norm_{k(Y)/k(Z)}a). SD applies over k(Z) to the generic lc fibre and its prime coefficient-one divisor S_η with parameter 1, bounding [k(Y):k(Z)] by fibre dimension. Thus q(deg h)(K_X+B) is principal with a uniform common multiple. Only one normalized component and its norm are used; no index theorem for reducible or nonnormal boundaries is assumed.

Lower-dimensional indices also bound finite volume characters. Suppose (V,E) is normal projective lc, E reduced, and a rational top form ω satisfies Div(ω)+E=0. A finite-order automorphism g acts by g^*ω=λω. On the quotient by g, finite adjunction produces coefficients 1−1/e outside E and 1 along E, lying in a fixed DCC set. An invariant power of ω gives rational triviality downstairs; L(dim V) gives a uniform principal multiple. Pulling back its generator yields a scalar multiple of ω to that power, so λ has uniformly bounded order. Dimension zero has character 1.

A finite map is quasi-étale if it is étale away from codimension at least two; étale means flat and unramified, locally like an invertible coordinate change over smooth complex varieties. If R→X is finite Galois quasi-étale and K_R∼0, a generating top form ω defines a character χ by g^*ω=χ(g)ω. Then mK_X∼0 exactly when χ^m=1. An invariant power descends as a rational pluriform with zero order at every prime divisor, trivializing the divisorial sheaf, including Cartierness. Conversely, a generator downstairs pulls back to an invariant generator. Thus a character bound controls the exact canonical index.

The cyclic cover attached to a primitive canonical trivialization of a klt variety with K_X∼_Q 0 is connected, quasi-étale, and has Cartier trivial canonical divisor. Its singularities are canonical. The singular Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition supplies a further cover A×B_1×...×B_s→X, where A is abelian and each B_i is an irreducible Calabi-Yau or irreducible holomorphic symplectic block. Höring-Peternell's decomposition uses Druel and Greb-Guenancia-Kebekus; the corrected versions of the latter are relevant.

An abelian variety is a projective algebraic group. A Calabi-Yau block and every connected normal quasi-étale cover of it have only constants and the generating top form in their reflexive form algebra. A symplectic block has form algebra generated by a nondegenerate two-form. Nonabelian blocks have no one-forms or forms in degree one below their dimension, hence no vector fields by contraction with a volume form. A Galois closure of a product cover need not itself be a product; that distinction cannot be discarded.

Nevertheless its factor directions can be recovered. For a geometrically integral normal finite quasi-étale cover R→B_i, the tangent sheaf splits into pulled-back factor directions E_i. There are no homomorphisms between different directions. Restriction to general slices gives connected quasi-étale covers of the nonabelian block; a cross-homomorphism would give a forbidden one-form or vector field. The factor projections are therefore central idempotents of End(T_R). Its primitive central idempotents number at most d=dim R because each contributes positive rank. A finite group permutes them, and a subgroup of index at most d! fixes every factor direction. A d!th power also fixes them.

For each factor, let F_i be the relative algebraic closure of k(B_i) in k(R). These are the common constants of derivations in the complementary directions. Their normalizations R_i→B_i are the Stein factors, so the subgroup preserves their fields. They remain geometrically integral because the function-field extensions are regular. Linear disjointness and divisorial ramification tests show that R_i→B_i and R→∏R_i are finite quasi-étale.

The induced factor transformations are regular, not merely birational. The map R→R_i has constant fibre dimension d−dim R_i. The two projections associated to a transformation map R onto its closed graph. A positive-dimensional fibre of the graph's first projection would create a larger fibre upstairs, impossible. The graph projection is finite birational, hence an isomorphism by normality; the inverse transformation is identical. The product of volume generators pulls back to a generator on R. All this works for semilinear transformations, which act on the ground field and send a factor to its field conjugate.
