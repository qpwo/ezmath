THE QUESTION, AND WHAT THIS ACCOUNT DOES

Can dimension alone tell us how far to look in the canonical equations
of an algebraic shape before we have found everything they can reveal?

The proposed answer is yes. A second proposed theorem uniformly bounds
certain canonical torsion orders. This account explains both statements,
their dependencies, and the calculations joining them. It is not an
independent verification of the claimed theorems.

A crucial assumption is the all-dimensional good-model theorem in
OpenAI's Log abundance in characteristic zero (LA, 2026). It includes
nonvanishing as well as abundance. Neither conclusion follows here
from finite generation for big adjoints. Further inputs come from
OpenAI's Arithmetic Stein-degree bounds for log Calabi-Yau pairs (SD),
Uniform effective log Iitaka fibrations for fourfolds (U4), and Relative
denominators and effective systems for log Calabi-Yau fibrations (R4).
The conclusion is conditional on the validity and applicability of
these inputs and of the intervening arguments.

The first three sections introduce the problem without assuming
algebraic geometry. Later sections retain the technical hypotheses
and proof calculations. Imported research theorems are identified,
not proved from school mathematics: a fully elementary proof is not
being claimed. No explicit, practical bound is obtained.
