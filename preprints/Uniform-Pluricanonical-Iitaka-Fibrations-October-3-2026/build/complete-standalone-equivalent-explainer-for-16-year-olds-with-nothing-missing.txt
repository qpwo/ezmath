Can the dimension of a shape defined by polynomial equations determine a single degree at which its canonical forms reveal all the information they can ever reveal?

The proposed answer is yes, over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. "Single degree" means a degree depending only on dimension, not on the equations, their coefficients, or the shape's other numerical invariants. The assertion is existential: no small, practical value is supplied.

The argument attributed to OpenAI, dated October 3, 2026, depends on substantial external results. In particular, OpenAI's Log abundance in characteristic zero (LA, 2026), Theorem 11.1, supplies good minimal models in every dimension, including nonvanishing. OpenAI's Arithmetic Stein-degree bounds for log Calabi–Yau pairs (SD, 2026) supplies an arithmetic degree bound. Uniform effective log Iitaka fibrations for fourfolds (U4, 2026) supplies dimension-independent chain and tracking lemmas and the preceding index method. Relative denominators and effective systems for log Calabi–Yau fibrations (R4, 2026) supplies the preceding relative method. The claimed conclusion is conditional on the validity and applicability of those inputs and the arguments joining them. In particular, all-dimensional abundance is not a consequence of finite generation for big adjoints. An elementary introduction to the objects does not turn these research inputs into elementary theorems.

A variety is a geometric object defined by polynomial equations. Its dimension counts independent local coordinates at a typical point. Integral means irreducible and reduced: one algebraic piece, without nilpotent thickening. Projective means closed in projective space, whose coordinates are ratios; projective complex varieties are compact. Smooth means that suitable local coordinates have no singularity. A field is algebraically closed when every nonconstant polynomial in one variable has a root. Characteristic zero means that adding 1 to itself never gives 0.

A rational function is a quotient of polynomial expressions, defined wherever its denominator does not vanish. The field k(X) consists of all rational functions on X. A birational map is a rational map with a rational inverse; it identifies dense open pieces and their function fields. A fibration groups points into fibres over a lower-dimensional base. Its generic fibre is the fibre over the base's function field; its geometric generic fibre additionally allows algebraic closure of that field. A contraction f:X→Z is projective and surjective, with f_*O_X=O_Z. Informally, functions constant along fibres come precisely from Z. For normal varieties in characteristic zero its generic fibre is geometrically integral.

At a smooth point of an n-dimensional variety, a top differential form looks like
    a(x) dx₁∧⋯∧dxₙ.
Changing coordinates multiplies the wedge by a Jacobian determinant. These local transformation rules define the canonical line bundle, denoted O_X(K_X). A line bundle is a family of one-dimensional vector spaces varying algebraically over X; a section chooses a vector in each fibre according to those rules. An m-canonical, or pluricanonical, form is a section of its mth tensor power O_X(mK_X). The integer m is its degree, not the degree of a polynomial equation defining X.

Write H⁰(X,mK_X) for the vector space of global m-canonical forms. A basis s₀,…,s_N defines the rational map
    x ↦ [s₀(x):⋯:s_N(x)].
This is the map of the complete system |mK_X|: every section in that degree is used. Where s₀ is nonzero, the map records the rational functions s_i/s₀. Changing the basis changes projective coordinates, not the field generated by these ratios.

If no positive degree has a nonzero section, the Kodaira dimension κ(X) is −∞. Otherwise κ(X) is the largest dimension of these images. Iitaka's theorem says that sufficiently large divisible degrees describe a stable fibration, the Iitaka fibration. Its function field K(K_X) is the subfield of k(X) generated by ratios of sections of the same positive degree, allowing all degrees. The desired uniform statement is:
    For each d≥1, some integer m(d)>0 makes |m(d)K_X| nonempty
    and its ratios generate K(K_X), for every smooth integral
    projective d-fold X with κ(X)≥0 in characteristic zero.

Equality of fields matters. The fields C(t²) and C(t) both describe one-dimensional images, but C(t²) misses t, and the extension C(t)/C(t²) has degree two. Merely obtaining an image of dimension κ(X) could likewise miss a finite part of the Iitaka field. If κ(X)=d, the uniform map must be birational. If κ(X)=0, it must be nonempty and have image a point.

The second assertion concerns divisors. A prime divisor is an irreducible subvariety of codimension one. A rational divisor is a finite sum D=Σ a_iD_i with rational coefficients. For a rational function h, Div(h) records its orders of zero and pole at prime divisors. Such a divisor is principal. Linear equivalence D∼E means D−E is principal; rational linear equivalence D∼_Q E means a positive integral multiple of D−E is principal. Numerical equivalence only compares intersection numbers and is weaker. A divisor can be numerically trivial without being torsion.

A Cartier divisor is locally the divisor of one rational function; it defines a line bundle. A rational Cartier divisor is a rational divisor with some positive integral Cartier multiple. On a normal variety, integral Weil divisors also have divisorial sheaves: their rational sections satisfy the required inequalities at every prime divisor, even when these sheaves are not line bundles. Normality ensures that regularity away from codimension-two subsets suffices for these divisorial tests. Reflexive differential forms similarly extend from the smooth locus across codimension at least two.

A resolution replaces singularities by a smooth variety through a proper birational map. An exceptional divisor is a divisor contracted to codimension at least two. Normalization repairs failures of integral closure without changing the function field; a normal variety already has integrally closed local rings. Q-factorial means every integral Weil divisor has a positive Cartier multiple. A small birational modification contracts no divisors. Pullback transports functions, forms, or Cartier divisors along a map; pushforward of divisors sends each surviving prime to its image with the appropriate degree and discards contracted primes. The symbols f* and f_* denote these opposite operations according to context.

Intersection numbers count intersections after moving representatives into suitable position, extended by linearity to rational divisors. For example, the degree of a line bundle on a curve counts the zeros minus poles of a rational section. The number D^e on an e-fold is the intersection of e copies of D. A boundary component is horizontal over a base if it dominates that base, and vertical otherwise. The notation ⌊D⌋ rounds each coefficient down. A torsion divisor class becomes zero after multiplication by some positive integer; the uniform problem asks for one integer for an entire class of varieties.

A pair (X,B) consists of a normal integral variety and an effective boundary B=Σ b_iB_i, with rational coefficients in [0,1] and K_X+B rational Cartier. On a resolution q:W→X, write
    K_W+B_W=q*(K_X+B).
For any prime E on any such model, its log discrepancy is 1−coeff_E(B_W). Log canonical, abbreviated lc, means all these numbers are nonnegative; Kawamata log terminal, klt, means they are positive. Divisorial log terminal, dlt, is the lc condition with a controlled simple-normal-crossing description around the generic points of its boundary strata. For zero boundary, canonical means exceptional discrepancies (log discrepancies minus one) are nonnegative, and terminal means they are positive. These conditions allow controlled singularities while keeping track of how forms behave under resolution.

The claimed index statement is:
    For each d≥0 and rational DCC set Φ⊂[0,1], some integer a(d,Φ)>0
    makes a(d,Φ)(K_X+B) an integral principal divisor for every
    projective normal lc d-fold pair with coefficients in Φ
    and K_X+B∼_Q0.
DCC, the descending chain condition, excludes an infinite strictly decreasing sequence. A DCC set need not be finite: {1−1/e:e≥1} is an example. A uniform index means a common trivializing multiple, clearing actual coefficients and killing the divisor class, not merely bounding a Cartier denominator. A bound on the possible least indices gives a common multiple by taking their least common multiple.

Positivity measures the supply of sections. A divisor is effective if its coefficients are nonnegative; pseudo-effective if its numerical class is a limit of effective classes; nef if it has nonnegative intersection with every curve; ample if a positive multiple embeds the variety in projective space; semiample if a positive multiple has no base points and therefore defines a morphism. A divisor D on an e-fold is big when its section count grows like a positive constant times k^e. Its volume is
    vol(D)=limsup e! h⁰(X,⌊kD⌋)/k^e.
For a nef big divisor this equals its top intersection D^e. The notation A≼B means B−A is rationally linearly equivalent to an effective divisor. Restricting this relation requires avoiding containment in that effective divisor.

Historically, Hacon–McKernan, Takayama, and Tsuji established uniform birational pluricanonical degrees in general type. Hacon–McKernan–Xu extended effective birationality to big lc adjoints with DCC boundary coefficients. Fujino–Mori's canonical bundle formula gave a uniform result for threefolds of Kodaira dimension one. Viehweg–Zhang treated two-dimensional Iitaka bases with dependence on fibre invariants. Birkar–Zhang gave a general effective Iitaka result depending on dimension, a least nonvanishing fibre degree, and a middle Betti number of a smooth model of the fibre's canonical cover. The extra difficulty below general type is controlling fibre torsion and monodromy, the transformation of cohomology around a degeneration. The proposed argument bounds the needed characters without assuming uniform bounds on nonabelian middle Betti numbers.

The induction has three assertions in dimension n: the effective Iitaka assertion I(n), the normal lc index assertion L(n), and its zero-boundary klt case K(n). Dimension zero needs degree one. Assuming I(j) and L(j) for j<n, lower-dimensional lc indices control moduli denominators and torsion over rationally connected bases. Separately, lower-dimensional effective Iitaka maps control section orders. These two routes combine to prove K(n). Adjunction and an arithmetic field norm then give L(n); moduli denominators and effective birationality on the base give I(n). The denominator argument must not use L(n) before it has been proved.

Hacon–McKernan–Xu's global ACC theorem reduces L(n) for a DCC coefficient set to L(n) for a finite subset: coefficients of numerically trivial lc pairs in fixed dimension cannot range freely through that DCC set. ACC is the ascending chain condition. Their ACC for log canonical thresholds also controls discriminant coefficients below. Kollár's Singularities of the Minimal Model Program fixes the discrepancy conventions; Kollár–Mori's Birational Geometry of Algebraic Varieties supplies adjunction and finite discrepancy formulas.

The good-model input LA says that a projective lc pair with effective rational boundary and pseudo-effective adjoint has a good log minimal model. Thus the adjoint has a nonzero multiple and becomes semiample on that model. Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan, abbreviated BCHM, supplies the klt minimal model program, extractions, and finite generation for big klt adjoints. A minimal model program contracts or flips negative directions. A flip changes the variety in codimension at least two while reversing the relevant relative positivity. A crepant change preserves the pullback of the full canonical adjoint.

A useful descent observation is elementary. If a rational function on a normal projective contraction X→Z has only vertical zeros and poles, its restriction to the generic fibre has none. It is therefore regular there and belongs to k(Z). If K_X+B∼_Q0 and q(K_F+B_F)∼0 on the generic fibre, extend a trivializing q-canonical form to a rational form s on X. Then
    Div(s)+qB=f*D,    D∼_Q0.
Indeed, a suitable power s^a divided by a global trivializing aq-log form is a rational function with vertical divisor. It comes from the base, and division of that base divisor by a gives D.

Linear triviality on a geometric generic fibre descends in the same degree. If a specified divisorial sheaf becomes an invertible trivial sheaf after algebraic closure, faithful flatness descends invertibility. Base change gives a one-dimensional space of sections over the original field; any nonzero descended section is a generator because it becomes one after extension. This avoids multiplying by an uncontrolled field-extension degree.

To deduce L(n) from K(n), Xu's klt induction theorem first handles klt pairs with boundary. For a non-klt pair, take a crepant Q-factorial dlt modification and put T=⌊B⌋≠0, adjoining 1 to the finite coefficient set. For small rational ε>0, the pair (X,B−εT) is klt and its adjoint is rationally equivalent to −εT, which is not pseudo-effective. Its MMP ends in a Mori fibre space, a contraction on which the negative perturbed adjoint is relatively ample.

The unperturbed K_X+B stays crepant through this program. A fixed equality r(K_X+B)=Div(h) pushes through contractions and flips; on common resolutions both pullbacks are Div(h)/r. Thus lc singularities and exact principal multiples persist even if dlt singularities do not. If the Mori base is a point, X is of Fano type. Birkar's bounded complement theorem supplies a bounded complement B⁺≥B with c(K_X+B⁺)∼0. The difference B⁺−B is effective and numerically trivial, hence zero by intersection with an ample class.

If the Mori base Z has positive dimension, T is relatively ample, so a component S dominates Z. Adjunction on its normalization ν:S^ν→S gives
    (K_X+B)|S^ν=K_S^ν+Θ.
The pair on S^ν is normal lc, effective, and rationally trivial. The coefficients of its different Θ lie in the rational DCC set determined by the original coefficients. Lower-dimensional L and global ACC give a uniform trivializing degree. Choose one q clearing the original coefficients and trivializing both this adjoint and the generic-fibre adjoint.

The descended form identity gives Div(s)+qB=f*D. Its q-fold residue along S is a nonzero rational q-canonical form v with
    Div(v)+qΘ=(fν)*D.
This is an equality of actual divisors: pluricanonical adjunction in a sufficiently divisible tensor power, followed by division of orders, proves it without assuming Cartierness in degree q everywhere on S. Divide v by a trivializing form v₀. In the Stein factorization
    S^ν → Y → Z,
the first map has connected fibres and the second, h, is finite. Vertical descent places a=v/v₀ in k(Y), and injectivity of divisor pullback gives h*D=Div_Y(a). The field norm then gives
    (deg h)D=Div_Z(Norm_{k(Y)/k(Z)}a).
SD applies over k(Z) to the generic lc fibre and its prime coefficient-one divisor S_η, with parameter 1. It bounds [k(Y):k(Z)] by fibre dimension. Thus q(deg h)(K_X+B) is principal with a uniform common multiple. Only one normalized component and its field norm are used; no index theorem for a reducible or nonnormal boundary is assumed.

Lower-dimensional indices also bound finite volume characters. Suppose (V,E) is normal projective lc, E reduced, and a rational top form ω satisfies Div(ω)+E=0. A finite-order automorphism γ acts by γ*ω=λω. On the quotient by γ, finite adjunction produces coefficients 1−1/e outside E and 1 along E. These lie in a fixed DCC set. An invariant power of ω gives rational triviality downstairs; L(dim V) gives a uniform principal multiple. Pulling its generator back yields a scalar multiple of ω to that power, so λ has uniformly bounded order. Dimension zero has character 1.

A finite map is quasi-étale if it is étale away from codimension at least two; étale means flat and unramified, with a morphism locally of finite presentation; over smooth complex varieties it behaves locally like an invertible change of coordinates. If R→X is finite Galois quasi-étale and K_R∼0, a generating top form ω defines a character χ by g*ω=χ(g)ω. Then
    mK_X∼0  ⇔  χ^m=1.
An invariant power descends as a rational pluriform with zero order at every prime divisor, hence trivializes the divisorial sheaf, including Cartierness. Conversely, a generator downstairs pulls back to an invariant generator. Thus a character bound controls the exact canonical index.

The cyclic cover attached to a primitive canonical trivialization of a klt variety with K_X∼_Q0 is connected, quasi-étale, and has Cartier trivial canonical divisor. Its singularities are canonical: klt discrepancies are greater than −1 and become integers. The singular Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition supplies a further cover
    A × B₁ × ⋯ × B_s → X,
where A is abelian and each B_i is an irreducible Calabi–Yau or irreducible holomorphic symplectic block. Höring–Peternell's decomposition uses the work of Druel and Greb–Guenancia–Kebekus. The corrected versions of the latter results are relevant.

An abelian variety is a projective algebraic group. A Calabi–Yau block and every connected normal quasi-étale cover of it have only constants and the generating top form in their reflexive form algebra. A symplectic block has its form algebra generated by a nondegenerate two-form. Nonabelian blocks have no one-forms or forms in degree dimension minus one, hence no vector fields by contraction with a volume form. A Galois closure of a product cover need not itself be a product; that distinction cannot be discarded.

Nevertheless its factor directions can be recovered. For a geometrically integral normal finite quasi-étale cover R→∏B_i, the tangent sheaf splits into pulled-back factor directions E_i. There are no homomorphisms between different directions. Restricting to general slices gives connected quasi-étale covers of a nonabelian block; a cross-homomorphism would give a forbidden one-form or vector field there. The factor projections are therefore central idempotents of End(T_R). Its primitive central idempotents number at most d=dim R because each contributes positive rank. A finite group permutes them, and a subgroup of index at most d! fixes every factor direction. A d!th power also fixes them.

For each factor, let F_i be the relative algebraic closure of k(B_i) in k(R). These are exactly the common constants of derivations in the complementary directions. Their normalizations R_i→B_i are the Stein factors, so the subgroup preserves their fields. They remain geometrically integral because the function-field extensions are regular. Linear disjointness with the other independent product fields and divisorial ramification tests show that R_i→B_i and R→∏R_i are finite quasi-étale.

The induced factor transformations are regular, not merely birational. The map R→R_i has constant fibre dimension d−dim R_i. The two projections associated to a transformation map R onto its closed graph. A positive-dimensional fibre of the graph's first projection would create a larger fibre upstairs, which is impossible. The graph projection is thus finite birational, hence an isomorphism by normality; the inverse transformation is treated identically. The product of volume generators pulls back to a generator on R. All of this works for semilinear transformations, which also act on the ground field and send a factor to its field conjugate.

For families over a curve, first define the geometric product cover after a finite extension of the curve field, take a Galois closure over the original total field, and replace the curve field by its relative algebraic closure in that closure. Regularity of the original total field makes the constants extension Galois. The resulting cover is geometrically integral over its constants; conjugation gives semilinear actions. Purity over the smooth locus preserves quasi-étaleness for conjugate covers and their Galois closure. This construction can be repeated after further curve extensions, without bounding their degrees.

Now let f:X→Z be a contraction from a klt n-fold, with dim Z>0 and K_X rationally linearly pulled back from Z, with zero boundary on X. Lower-dimensional L gives a fixed fibre trivializing degree p₀. Choose ψ so that p₀K_X+Div(ψ) is vertical. Comparing with an initial rational pullback and using vertical descent gives an exact equality
    K_X+(1/p₀)Div(ψ)=f*D_Z,
    D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z.
Ambro's klt-trivial canonical bundle formula supplies the effective discriminant B_Z and moduli b-divisor M. A b-divisor records compatible divisors on birational models. It is b-nef and b-Cartier when it descends to a nef Cartier divisor on one such model. A generalized pair includes this higher-model nef data when computing discrepancies.

Ambro's generic rank-one condition holds because the rounded discrepancy divisor on a resolution over the generic base point is effective exceptional; allowing poles there adds no rational functions. At a prime P of Z the coefficient of B_Z is 1−t_P, where t_P is the lc threshold of f*P. Since the total-space boundary is zero, 0<t_P≤1. Threshold ACC puts these coefficients in a rational DCC set depending only on n. The base is generalized klt. Qualitative moduli theory already gives a smooth model W where M descends as a nef rational Cartier divisor; the remaining task is a uniform integer p, divisible by p₀, making pM_W Cartier.

Fix a prime on W, let α be its coefficient in the pullback of D_Z, and let t be its crepant threshold. Since canonical divisors are integral, clearing α+t clears the corresponding coefficient of M_W. Resolve and cut W by general ample hypersurfaces to a transverse smooth curve C with marked point c. Bertini preserves the finitely many resolution strata and their threshold calculation, while the cuts avoid relevant codimension-two base subsets. Choose adjunction canonical representatives using linearly equivalent cutting divisors avoiding c. Their rational-function corrections have coefficient zero at c. The sliced equality is
    K_V+B^c+(1/p₀)Div(ψ_C)=h*D_C,    coeff_c(D_C)=α.
The geometric generic fibre remains birational to the original zero-boundary klt fibre. A horizontal boundary has not been introduced into that generic model.

A divisorial valuation measures the order of zero or pole along a prime divisor on some birational model. A uniformizer is a local function vanishing to order one along the divisor or marked curve point. The lc threshold of a divisor is the largest amount that can be added to the boundary while keeping the required discrepancies nonnegative. A residue extracts the form left on a boundary component after removing its logarithmic normal factor dz/z. Simple normal crossings means that locally the boundary is a union of distinct coordinate hyperplanes. Semistable reduction seeks a model whose special fibre has precisely this reduced crossing form.

For a parameter z at c and a degree-m relative pluriform ω, define
    w_z(ω)=inf_E [ord_E(ω∧dz/z)+m]/[m ord_E(z)],
where E runs over vertical divisorial valuations. Tensor powers are understood in the wedge notation; the order is the ordinary canonical order. If θ represents K_V, the generic generator
    φ=(θ/dz)^p₀ ψ_C
has
    w_z(φ)=α−1+t.
Indeed, a crepant coefficient b_E contributes α−1+(1−b_E)/ord_E(z), and the infimum of the last terms is the threshold.

Three weight rules follow from the ramification formula. A finite extension of the fibre field with the base fixed leaves the weight unchanged: numerator and denominator both multiply by the valuation's ramification index. A base extension z=u^l times a unit multiplies weight by l. Tensor powers leave weight unchanged, and multiplying a degree-m form by a base function a adds ord_z(a)/m. Divisorial valuations restrict and prolong correctly; in a Galois curve extension the branches are conjugate, so the infimum is independent of the chosen branch.

After suitable curve extensions, compare the generic fibre with its product blocks and a Galois cover R. Put b=n!. An inertia generator g, the cyclic local action around the marked point, has a finite-order lift to R, and g^b preserves all Stein block directions. Use projective equivariant resolutions and semistable reduction to obtain smooth block total spaces with reduced simple-normal-crossing special fibres. Kempf–Knudsen–Mumford–Saint-Donat's Toroidal Embeddings I supplies semistable reduction. Equivariance is retained by barycentric boundary subdivision, equivariant root extraction and normalization, and compatible projective subdivisions on the quotient combinatorial complex, pulled back using the original cone lattices rather than geometric quotient lattices. Further ramification may be arbitrarily large. Cyclic inertia surjects down the tower, allowing compatible generators.

On each semistable block, a degree-one generic volume generator η_i has integral weight. Multiply by a power of the final parameter u to normalize w_u(η_i)=0. Its logarithmic top form is regular and has order zero on some component; the lc crossing calculation makes all further valuation tests nonnegative. The product of normalized forms also has weight zero. Locally the product models have equations ∏x_ij=u. Relative logarithmic generators multiply without an extra u-power; the saturated toroidal fibre product has reduced special fibre, and its toroidal resolution retains the generators. At a product of zero-order generic components the product has order zero. Finite-cover invariance transfers this to R even though R need not split.

The pulled-back generator therefore has the form
    φ=a(∧η_i)^p₀,    l w_z(φ)=ord_u(a)/p₀,
where a is a base function and l is the total base ramification. An empty product covers a zero-dimensional fibre. Write
    (g^b)*η_i=c_iη_i,    λ_i=c_i(c).
Equal weights make c_i a unit, and finite order makes λ_i a root of unity. If g acts on u to first order by the primitive lth root ζ, invariance of φ gives
    ζ^[b ord_u(a)] ∏λ_i^p₀=1.
Thus an integer N killing every λ_i implies
    l divides bN ord_u(a),    p₀bN w_z(φ) is integral.
The objective is to bound these characters, not l.

Cohomology records global information that local formulas alone can miss. H⁰ consists of global sections; higher H^j measure further global compatibility, and integral topological cohomology also detects holes. A Betti number is its rank over the integers after removing torsion. An action on a one-dimensional space multiplies a generator by a scalar, called its character. A root of unity has finite multiplicative order. An integral matrix with a primitive eth root as an eigenvalue must contain all its algebraic conjugates, so its size is at least the degree φ(e) of the cyclotomic polynomial. Unipotent means that every eigenvalue is one.

For an abelian block of dimension s<n, Deligne's canonical extension and Steenbrink's semistable Hodge comparison identify the normalized form with a frame of the extended top Hodge line. The integral cohomology in degree s has rank binomial(2s,s). Combining the finite action with parallel transport gives an integral matrix commuting with unipotent monodromy; a power is a monodromy power. Passing to canonical-extension frames multiplies it by a commuting unipotent factor, which does not change eigenvalues. If λ has order e, its cyclotomic polynomial has degree φ(e)≤binomial(2s,s), where φ is Euler's totient function. Only finitely many orders satisfy this bound.

For a nonabelian block, construct an equivariant good special-fibre model. Begin with a smooth semistable compactification Q and quotient by the finite cyclic action. Its branch boundary Δ satisfies K_Q=pullback(K_(Q/G)+Δ), and the quotient pair is klt and Q-factorial. A generic pluricanonical form, multiplied over its translates, shows relative pseudo-effectivity. Add the pullback of a sufficiently high-degree divisor on the quotient curve, represented by many general points with small coefficients, preserving klt singularities and making the adjoint absolutely pseudo-effective. Take its degree greater than 2 dim Q. LA gives a good model and a terminating MMP with scaling. The extremal-ray length bound forces every step to be over the curve: a horizontal negative curve would contradict that degree inequality.

Lift the steps by normalization and Stein factorization. Finite pullback preserves the negativity and relative ampleness for contractions and flips; Prokhorov's equivariant MMP formalizes the construction. No divisors are extracted. The resulting Y is klt, its fibre T is reduced Cartier, (Y,T) is dlt, and K_Y is relatively semiample. Fibre multiplicities stay one. Klt implies Cohen–Macaulay, so generic reducedness of the Cartier fibre excludes embedded components and gives scheme-theoretic reducedness.

The effective logarithmic divisor of η∧du/u persists on Y. The generic fibre has Kodaira dimension zero, so the semiample contraction has the curve itself as its Stein image. Thus K_Y+T is rationally trivial near T. Its effective log divisor has no horizontal part and, by vertical descent, equals aT for a≥0. If a>0, every weight would be positive, contradicting normalization. Hence
    Div_log(η∧du/u)=0.
This is a degree-one generator: K_Y+T and K_Y are Cartier near T. Adjunction makes T Gorenstein with trivial dualizing line. On a preserved prime component S, residue generates K_S+Θ_S. Dlt adjunction gives a normal lc pair; integrality of the degree-one form's divisor makes Θ_S integral, hence reduced. The residue character is the corresponding power of λ.

A bounded power preserves a component. The family is flat, its central fibre is semi-log-canonical, and nearby fibres are klt; these fibres are Du Bois. Kollár's Families of Varieties of General Type gives constancy of h^j(O) in this proper flat setting. Rational singularities, extension of forms by Greb–Kebekus–Kovács–Peternell, and Hodge symmetry identify these dimensions with the block's form algebra. For a Calabi–Yau block the only nonzero groups are one-dimensional in degrees 0 and s, so the alternating trace is 1+(−1)^sλ^−1, nonzero if λ has order greater than two. For a symplectic block only even degrees contribute. Among powers of exponent at most s+1, some trace is nonzero: otherwise Newton's identities applied to the nonzero eigenvalues would force their product to vanish.

Donovan's coherent Lefschetz formula implies that a finite-order action with nonzero alternating trace has a fixed point, including on the possibly singular projective fibre. At most s+1 dlt boundary components meet there. A further power dividing (s+1)! preserves one component. Its dimension is s<n, so the earlier finite log-volume character bound applies to its reduced lc pair. This bounds λ. Taking common multiples over all block dimensions and types gives N(n). Consequently p=p₀n!N(n) clears every coefficient of M_W. Smoothness of W turns integrality into Cartierness, proving the uniform moduli denominator.

There are two uses of that denominator. First suppose D_Z is big. On a smooth determination τ:W→Z, let Γ be the strict transform of B_Z plus the reduced exceptional divisor. Generalized klt gives
    K_W+Γ+M_W=τ*D_Z+E,    E≥0 exceptional.
Birkar–Zhang's polarized effective birationality theorem applies to this big log-smooth adjoint with a fixed DCC boundary set and fixed nef Cartier denominator. A uniform rounded system is birational. Its sections push down because the divisors agree at nonexceptional prime points. Taking a common multiple makes its degree m divisible by p₀ without losing its ratios.

The exact divisor equality, not just rational equivalence, then gives
    H⁰(X,mK_X)=ψ^(m/p₀) f*H⁰(Z,⌊mD_Z⌋).
A base section pulls back effectively. Conversely, division by the displayed common factor makes an upstairs section a regular function on the projective generic fibre, hence an element of C(Z). Orders above base primes give Div(h)+mD_Z≥0, equivalent to the rounded inequality. The uniform upstairs ratios therefore generate all of C(Z).

Second, consider a generalized klt d-fold (Z,B+M_Z) with D=K_Z+B+M_Z∼_Q0, B in a fixed rational DCC set, pM b-Cartier, and a rationally connected smooth resolution. Rationally connected means that two general points can be joined by a rational curve. There is a uniform ℓ making the actual divisor ℓD integral principal. Two separate issues must be addressed: clearing coefficients and killing class-group torsion.

Generalized global ACC first makes the original coefficients finite and the generalized log discrepancies uniformly at least ε>0. The necessary extractions can be justified with ordinary klt pairs. On a high resolution g:W→Z, write K_W+B_W+M_W=g*D and choose an effective exceptional Cartier F with −F relatively ample. A jet is a truncated Taylor expansion: keeping terms through degree a amounts to reducing modulo the (a+1)st power of the maximal ideal, the ideal of functions vanishing at the point. A blowup replaces a point by its tangent directions, with an exceptional divisor recording vanishing order. In characteristic p, the Frobenius map raises functions to their pth powers; the binomial identity (x+y)^p=x^p+y^p makes it algebraic. The relative Frobenius targets a base-field twist, denoted W', so that the map respects the chosen ground-field structure. The diagonal in a product consists of tuples with all coordinates equal. A maximal minor is the determinant of a square submatrix of full target size; its nonvanishing detects full rank.

Three elementary estimates support that comparison. For a rank-ρ bundle E on a smooth curve of genus g in characteristic p, assume its minimum Harder–Narasimhan slope is nonnegative. A slope is degree divided by rank; the Harder–Narasimhan filtration orders the semistable pieces by decreasing slope. Put G₀=max(0,2g−2), u=ρ(p−1), c₂=ρ(ρ−1)G₀. The truncated symmetric algebra
    A(E)=O[v₁,…,v_ρ]/(v₁^p,…,v_ρ^p)
is independent of frame, has total rank p^ρ, and satisfies
    μ_max(A_j(E))≤(p−1)deg(E)+c₂,    0≤j≤u.

Langer's canonical-connection method supplies a direct proof without assuming tensor semistability in characteristic p. For every rank-ρ U,
    μ_min(F*U)≥p μ_min(U)−(ρ−1)G₀.
A slope gap exceeding G₀ makes the second fundamental map of the last such filtration step vanish; the step descends under Frobenius. In a generic matrix chart with an identity minor, connection invariance forces all other entries to have derivative zero, hence to be pth powers. Saturation and flatness give the descended subbundle. The quotient's average slope is at least p μ_min(U), and its remaining gaps are at most G₀, proving the inequality.

Iteration bounds μ_min((F^e)*E) below by −(ρ−1)G₀(p^e−1)/(p−1). Twist by a line bundle of degree between 2g−μ_min and 2g+1−μ_min to make it globally generated, using Serre duality after subtracting a point. The chain lemma gives positive-dimensional leaves H_t with
    P_t^h_t H_t≤(h_tB)^h_t.
Their finite images W_t in V^t are geometrically integral generic leaves of rational quotients ξ_t. Projection sends upper leaves into lower leaves: the lower quotient's constancy on a generic projected movement remains an identity after retaining the entire upper-chain history. Stationary projected steps also preserve it. All comparisons use generic tuples over a common algebraically closed extension of the quotient parameter fields.

For fixed T and large r, every H_t projects generically finitely onto its image in each V-slot. Otherwise let its slot image have dimension j and generic fibre dimension a=h_t−j>0. The ambient very general Seshadri bound gives L^j·image≥c^j. Over a chosen lift of the generic slot point, the fibre of Z_t→V is Y^(t−1); its restricted polarization has Seshadri constant at least cr^(1/n), by curve projection. A generic a-dimensional fibre component therefore has degree at least (cr^(1/n))^a. These subvariety bounds follow by intersecting the nef class on the blowup at the smooth generic mark. Projection formula and nefness give
    P_t^h_t H_t≥c^j(cr^(1/n))^a,
contradicting the bounded chain degree as r grows. Hence 1≤h_t≤n.

Let d_t be the degree of W_t over its first-slot image. The preceding bounds give
    d_t≤K T^(2n),
    K=max_(1≤h≤n)(h(C₂+1)/c)^h,
independent of T. Projection compatibility and slot finiteness make h_t nonincreasing. When h_t=h_(t−1), the projection is onto the lower integral leaf and
    d_t/d_(t−1)=deg(W_t→W_(t−1))
is a positive integer. A constant stretch of dimensions has length at least (T−1)/n. Choose T with
    (T−1)/n−1>log₂(KT^(2n)).
If every adjacent degree on that stretch were at least two, exponential growth would exceed the polynomial bound. Therefore some k≥3 has h_k=h_(k−1)>0 and a birational coordinate-forgetting map W_k→W_(k−1). Symmetry gives the same for every omitted slot.

At a generic tuple, the leaf tangent distribution D_k=ker(dξ_k) projects injectively into every slot and isomorphically to the lower distribution under each omission. Let A_i be its slot image. Rational Grassmann coordinates show A_i depends only on x_i: it belongs to the function field of every containing (k−1)-tuple, whose intersection is the single-slot field. Similarly transport between A_i and A_j depends only on (x_i,x_j). For independent product variables this field-intersection statement follows by replacing omitted variables with fresh independent variables and then specializing those to general constants.

Symmetry gives a rational plane field A(x) and transport R(x,y):A(x)→A(y). Three slots give
    R(y,z)R(x,y)=R(x,z).
Choose a general reference point and transport a basis. This yields h>0 independent rational vector fields v₁,…,v_h whose simultaneous copies span D_k. Commutativity is neither claimed nor needed.

The missing coordinate is recoverable rationally from the others and the quotient value. The map
    Λ=(first k−1 coordinates, ξ_k):V^k→D
to its image closure is birational. Its function-field extension is finite, and regularity over the quotient field, from geometric integrality of the generic leaf, preserves its degree after algebraic closure. The geometric forgetting map has degree one, so that degree is already one. Write ev for the last coordinate of the rational inverse.

On suitable smooth complex opens, compose small-time holomorphic flows of v₁,…,v_h in a fixed order, calling the composition E_z. The reverse order with negative times gives its local inverse. Simultaneous flows preserve ξ_k. For a fixed general (k−1)-tuple u and y near a general y₀,
    E_z(y)=ev(E_z(u),ξ_k(u,y)).
For fixed small z the right side is rational in y. Equality on a nonempty analytic open, which is Zariski dense, verifies its domain and gives a rational self-map. Applying the same argument to inverse flows gives a rational inverse. Since h>0 and the vector fields are independent at y₀, the map z↦E_z(y₀) has nonzero derivative and uncountable image. This contradicts countability of Bir(V).

The impossible sequence proves K(n). For n=1, a normal projective curve with torsion canonical class is a smooth genus-one curve, whose canonical index is one. The normal-boundary reduction then proves L(n), and global ACC gives its full rational DCC form.

To finish I(n), start with smooth X and κ(X)≥0. LA and the klt MMP give a terminal good minimal model V. Canonical sections agree in every integer degree:
    H⁰(X,mK_X)=H⁰(V,mK_V),    m≥0,
where the right side is divisorial even if mK_V is not Cartier. On a common resolution q:W→V, canonicality gives K_W=q*K_V+A with A≥0 exceptional. A divisorial m-canonical section has an effective rational Cartier zero divisor on V, since it differs from mK_V by a principal divisor. Pullback and addition of mA make it regular on W. Conversely, regularity on W implies the prime-divisor tests on V. These are actual integral orders of forms and impose no extra divisibility on m. The smooth model X has the same comparison, preserving ratios.

Semiampleness gives the Iitaka contraction f:V→Z with K_V∼_Q f*D and D rational ample. If Z is a point, L(n) supplies a common nonempty degree. Otherwise the zero-boundary denominator result and big-base birationality give a degree depending only on n whose ratios generate C(Z). The all-degree comparison transfers it to X. There are finitely many base dimensions; take a common multiple of their degrees and the point-case degree.

Passing to a multiple preserves the full field. If s₀≠0 has degree m, then in degree qm,
    s/s₀=(s s₀^(q−1))/s₀^q.
Thus the old ratios remain. All new ratios still belong to the Iitaka field by definition. This closes the simultaneous induction, and every positive multiple of m(n) also works.

Finally let k be any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Descend the finite defining data to a finitely generated subfield k₀, take its algebraic closure inside k, and embed that algebraic closure into C. Singularity conditions, dimensions, section spaces, Cartierness, and triviality are preserved under algebraically closed extensions, using resolutions, base change, and faithful flatness. For the index assertion include a rational principalization of some sufficiently divisible adjoint multiple among the data. The uniform complex trivialization descends by the one-dimensional-section argument and extends to k.

For the Iitaka assertion include a nonzero pluricanonical section to preserve κ≥0. Compare the uniform degree m(n) with a sufficiently large divisible degree r that is a multiple of m(n) and already defines the Iitaka fibration. Products give a dominant rational map from the degree-r image to the degree-m(n) image. After comparison with C it is birational. Birationality, equivalently degree one of the function-field extension, is detected by faithfully flat algebraically closed extension, so it holds over k. The same dimension-dependent integers work over every such field.
